Connecta AI compares what Google shows with what the business most likely does on each special day (public holidays, eves, school breaks and local events) and suggests hours when it expects to be right more often than wrong. Nothing is published until the owner answers.

## Where the suggestions come from

Clues propose hours and report their evidence: the business's own past entries for the same holiday, its shopping centre's hours, what similar businesses do, how often its type of business closes, and what customers say in reviews. A model trained on earlier seasons and earlier owners' answers weighs them. Most of the time it stays quiet: businesses mostly keep their normal hours, and a wrong "closed" costs as much as a right one earns. The reasons shown on each suggestion ("Why we suggest this") are the evidence behind it.

## How sure, and how important

Each suggestion has a confidence (how sure the predictor is) and an importance (High, Medium, Low), worked out from the confidence, how many Google views the same day had last season, and how soon it is (within a week counts double, within a month a quarter more). Importance is relative to the business's top upcoming suggestion. The most important one is shown first.

## Answering

Approve publishes the suggested hours for that date. Edit publishes your own hours for it instead. Decline keeps the hours as they are, and those hours aren't suggested again for that holiday. The same three answers are on WhatsApp, Telegram, the email's link and the dashboard; the first answer counts.

## What an answer changes

Every answer makes the predictor look at the business again within seconds. Approved or edited hours count as the owner's own word, so they change how likely the other special days are too. For example, confirming Christmas Day closed can make Epiphany closed more likely.

## Linked pages

If a page linked under Company information says something else for the same date, the suggestion shows a warning, isn't sent in messages, and isn't applied automatically under standing permission. Check the page before answering.

## The closed-for-good question

When customers say the business has shut down, the owner is asked once whether it's closed for good. Confirming goes to a person at Fonecta, who checks before the listing is taken down; it's never automatic. Answer it on its card on the front page.

## No history yet

For a business that has never entered special hours, the predictor uses how often the same type of business closes in the same country, what similar businesses entered, recent reviews, and seasons. It suggests only when that evidence is strong enough.
